The effect appears only where an LPI can act

Turning collisions where the pedestrian had right-of-way fell 37%. Collision types an LPI cannot prevent didn't move. A citywide trend would have moved every row.

The drop follows installation

Flat before installation (joint pre-trend test p = 0.74), down after, and the reduction persists. Regression to the mean would fade, not deepen.

Collision rates, treated vs never-treated sites

Raw rates, no model. Treated sites start slightly worse, since the busier sites are the ones that get chosen, and end better. The models exist to turn this picture into a number.

Who benefits, and by how much

The head start is worth most to those who cross slowest, with a 42% drop for pedestrians over 65. The orange Empirical Bayes row corrects for where Toronto chose to install, and is the planning number.

The numbers to take away

Toronto installs LPIs where collisions just happened, so the Empirical Bayes 0.89, not the raw −25.8%, is the number for planning. The aggregate effect does not survive multiplicity correction; the mechanism and older-pedestrian results do.
